## Public-facing summary (review draft)
In October 1808, a large group of enslaved people and Khoekhoe labourers organised an uprising in the Cape Colony, then under British colonial administration. Led in part by Louis of Mauritius and Abraham of the Cape, they moved through farms in the Swartland and Koeberg districts, seeking to free enslaved workers and advance towards Cape Town. Colonial forces stopped them near Salt River. The uprising was suppressed and leaders were severely punished. It remains an important example of enslaved people's own organised resistance.

## Material uncertainty — never hide it
- **27 October 1808:** Nigel Worden's article (GroundUp, 30 March 2016), and South African History Online articles, give 27 October. SAHO's account describes assembly at a farm on the evening of the 27th.
- **25 October 1808:** Cape Town Museum and Camissa Museum identify 25 October as the beginning of the Jij Rebellion.
- **Numbers:** Worden reports *about 340* enslaved people. Other museum summaries report *over 326*, alongside Khoekhoe labourers and two Irish sailors. Avoid precision greater than the evidence supports.
- Proposed chronology label: **October 1808; disputed onset (25/27 October)**. Do not assert 27 October is an official commemoration or invent a single certain date.
