## 3. Live sports input

Live sports input means public-safe operational sports signals only.

Allowed inputs:

- official fixtures
- official results
- league tables
- match times
- venue availability
- event status
- cancellations
- weather risk for outdoor activity
- crowd/safety notices
- safeguarding alerts
- team registrations
- coach/volunteer approval status
- media release status
- rights proof for photos, videos and highlights

Blocked inputs:

- covert tracking
- private player location tracking
- child profiling
- gambling odds as a foundation
- injury exploitation
- harassment signals
- unverified emergency claims
- fake official sports authority claims
- fake FIFA/FA/premier league affiliation claims

## 4. Adaptive learning

SMI may learn from certified sports records only.

Learning signals:

- which competitions attract real signups
- fixture attendance
- cancellations and why
- safeguarding incidents
- weather disruption
- age group demand
- gender category demand
- venue reliability
- team reliability
- coach reliability
- matchday operations
- community feedback
- player development pathways
- media engagement
- sponsor/local business response

SMI may propose improvements, but cannot independently create events, accept minors, publish risky claims, collect sensitive data, spend money, book venues or dispatch people.

## 5. Age and gender structure

OAP Sports must support:

- Seeds: 0-6 non-competitive movement/play only
- Explorers: 7-9 fun development
- Learners: 10-12 foundation skills
- Builders: 13-15 structured youth sport
- Creators: 16-18 youth competition with safeguarding
- Leaders: 19-21 development league
- Stewards: 22-24 emerging adult pathway
- Adults: 25+
- Veterans / Legends where useful

Categories may include boys, girls, men, women and mixed/community formats where lawful, safe and sport-appropriate.

Youth safeguarding is mandatory. No cash pressure, no gambling, no exploitative scouting, no unsafe messaging.

Each ladder stage must have:

- eligibility rules

- safeguarding rules
- fixture rules
- result verification
- dispute process
- venue safety check
- HRM event record
- Guardian check
- Human Authority approval before launch

## 7. OAP Sports Distribution first

Recommended War Room path:

Build OAP Sports Distribution first before buying or building an owned field.

Why:

- lower cost
- faster launch
- can use partner/community venues
- proves demand before capital spending
- builds audience, teams, fixtures, tables and media
- creates HRM records needed to justify owned facilities
- avoids venue liability before operations are mature

OAP-owned sports field becomes a later milestone only after demand, compliance, safeguarding, insurance, finance, venue operations and local authority checks are proven.

## 8. Owned sports field gate

OAP should consider its own sports field only when all gates pass:

- at least one league running consistently
- real fixture demand proven
- venue booking cost justifies ownership/lease
- safeguarding policy active
- public liability route identified
- first aid/event safety route identified
- youth permissions process active
- accessibility plan active
- maintenance budget known
- local planning/lease/legal route reviewed
- War Room approves
- Human Authority approves

Until then: Sports Distribution is green path; owned field is amber/future.

## 10. War Room sports checks

Before any real-life event:

- Is the event legal and safe?
- Is the venue confirmed?
- Is the age group appropriate?
- Are minors protected?
- Are coaches/volunteers approved?
- Is first aid/safety covered?
- Is weather risk checked?
- Is media permission clear?
- Are rules understandable?
- Are disputes handled?
- Is the event public wording safe?
- Has HRM recorded the approval?
- Has Human Authority approved?
